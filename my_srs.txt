## 2. Introduction

### 2.1 Purpose
The purpose of this SRS is to define complete, testable, and traceable requirements for a medical monitoring platform that acquires, processes, visualizes, stores, alarms on, and exports physiological, hemodynamic, respiratory, perfusion, neuro-monitoring, and analytics-derived patient data. The specification is intended to guide software developers, firmware and embedded engineers, clinical workflow designers, UI/UX teams, cybersecurity engineers, testers, and regulatory documentation authors.

### 2.2 Scope
The platform covered by this SRS includes bedside patient monitoring software, sensor interface logic, parameter acquisition services, alarm management, local data persistence, user authentication and authorization, historical review, analytics display, connectivity interfaces, and optional enterprise integration services. The scope includes proof-of-concept implementation intended for monitored clinical environments such as operating rooms, procedural areas, post-anesthesia care units, and intensive care units.

The scope excludes final marketed labeling claims, certified interoperability declarations, final production manufacturing controls, and formal clinical performance claims requiring separate validation. The document nevertheless defines requirements in a manner suitable for eventual maturation into design input documentation.

### 2.3 Intended Use
The intended use of the platform is to display and manage patient physiological and hemodynamic monitoring information from connected sensors and associated calculation modules to support clinician awareness, trend assessment, alarm handling, and decision support in monitored healthcare settings. The platform is not intended, within this proof-of-concept scope, to autonomously diagnose disease or initiate therapy without clinician review.

### 2.4 Intended Users
Intended users include:
- Physicians in perioperative, critical care, and sedation environments.
- Nurses and nurse anesthetists responsible for bedside monitoring.
- Perfusionists, advanced hemodynamic specialists, and procedural teams.
- Biomedical engineering personnel responsible for setup, calibration, and troubleshooting.
- Clinical researchers and human factors evaluators operating the system in controlled pilot environments.

### 2.5 Clinical Environment
The system is intended for use in hospital and procedure-based settings with trained healthcare operators, line-powered equipment, controlled networking, and the possibility of multiple simultaneous patient monitoring sessions across different acuity levels. Environmental assumptions include high cognitive load, intermittent urgent interventions, glare, ambient noise, infection-control cleaning, and frequent alarm competition from adjacent systems.

### 2.6 Product Overview
The proof-of-concept product is a modular monitoring platform composed of a central software application, local acquisition and integration services, hardware abstraction interfaces for supported sensors, signal quality and validation services, alarm processing services, visualization components, historical trend review modules, predictive analytics views, and secure data exchange interfaces. Standard SRS frameworks place product perspective, functions, user classes, interfaces, constraints, and dependencies within the overall description; this document extends those principles to the medical monitoring domain.[cite:2][cite:5]

### 2.7 Clinical Context
The platform is intended to support continuous and intermittent observation of cardiovascular, respiratory, temperature, and neuro-monitoring parameters. It may also present advanced hemodynamic and predictive indices including cardiac output, cardiac index, stroke volume, stroke volume variation, pulse pressure variation, tissue oxygenation, oxygen delivery, hypotension prediction index, guided hemodynamic indicators, autoregulation or perfusion-related derived metrics, awareness or sedation metrics, and waveform-based interpretations where supported by sensor and algorithm availability.

### 2.8 System Boundaries
The system boundary includes:
- Local user application software.
- Sensor acquisition services and device communication modules.
- Platform operating system services required for execution.
- Optional edge gateway and optional cloud synchronization functions.
- Local audit logs, trend storage, alarm storage, and session records.
- Enterprise integration connectors for EMR or hospital information exchange.

The system boundary excludes:
- Therapy delivery hardware.
- Hospital network core infrastructure.
- Third-party EMR systems beyond defined interfaces.
- Clinician medical judgment.
- Non-supported third-party analytics engines.

### 2.9 Assumptions
- Use occurs under trained supervision by licensed clinical personnel.
- Connected sensors are applied according to manufacturer instructions.
- The PoC system is deployed in controlled evaluation environments.
- Network connectivity may be intermittent and cannot be assumed for essential local monitoring.
- Alarm annunciation on the local platform remains primary even when remote notifications are enabled.
- Predictive and advanced analytics are decision-support functions and shall not suppress primary physiological alarm behavior.

### 2.10 Dependencies
- Availability of compatible sensors or simulated sensor feeds.
- Availability of hardware abstraction layer drivers.
- Availability of validated time synchronization source.
- Availability of secure credential management service.
- Availability of operating system and cryptographic libraries maintained under controlled configuration.
- Availability of clinical workflow definitions and alarm policy configuration for deployment sites.

## 5. System Overview

### 5.1 High-Level Architecture
The platform shall use a modular layered architecture composed of acquisition interfaces, signal processing services, alarm and rules engines, parameter calculation modules, UI presentation components, persistence services, integration services, and cybersecurity services. A modular architecture with explicit interfaces supports traceability, verification planning, and controlled software partitioning consistent with medical device software lifecycle expectations.[cite:3][cite:5]

### 5.2 Textual Architecture Diagram
```text
[Patient Sensors / Simulators]
        |
        v
[Sensor Interface Drivers / Device Adapters]
        |
        v
[Hardware Abstraction Layer]
        |
        v
[Acquisition Manager] --> [Signal Quality Engine] --> [Parameter Calculation Engine]
        |                          |                        |
        |                          v                        v
        |                    [Technical Faults]       [Derived Metrics]
        |                          |                        |
        v                          v                        v
[Alarm Manager] <------------ [Clinical Rules Engine] ---- [Predictive Analytics Engine]
        |
        +--> [Audible Alarm Controller]
        +--> [Visual Alarm Controller]
        +--> [Notification Service]
        |
        v
[UI Layer: Dashboard / Trends / Waveforms / Settings / Pairing / Review]
        |
        +--> [Audit Log Service]
        +--> [Session Manager]
        +--> [Local Database]
        +--> [Export Service]
        +--> [HL7/FHIR Integration]
        +--> [Cloud / Remote Sync (Optional)]
```

### 5.3 Software Subsystems
- Acquisition Manager: receives raw data packets, timestamps them, performs source attribution, and forwards them for validation.
- Signal Quality Engine: evaluates availability, integrity, plausibility, and noise conditions.
- Parameter Calculation Engine: computes direct and derived numerical metrics.
- Alarm Manager: evaluates thresholds, priority rules, latching behavior, silence state, pause state, escalation rules, and alarm history.
- Clinical Rules Engine: coordinates dependencies among parameters, context, and monitoring mode.
- Predictive Analytics Engine: calculates supportive risk or trend indicators where validated inputs are present.
- UI Layer: presents live values, waveforms, alarms, trends, status indicators, prompts, and workflows.
- Persistence and Export Services: store records and support retrieval and export.
- Security Services: authenticate users, authorize actions, protect sessions, and record auditable events.

### 5.4 Hardware Architecture
The hardware environment may include a bedside computing platform, integrated or external touchscreen display, speakers or audio output, optional battery backup, communication interfaces for sensors and gateways, and optional network interfaces for enterprise integration. The hardware abstraction layer shall isolate platform-specific device communication details from clinical application logic.

### 5.5 Sensor Ecosystem
The platform may interface with invasive pressure sensors, arterial waveform sources, pulse oximetry probes, capnography sensors, temperature probes, EEG/EMG-based sedation modules, tissue oxygenation sensors, cardiac output measurement interfaces, and external patient monitor feeds. Supported sensor combinations shall be mode-dependent and shall be validated at connection time.

### 5.6 Connectivity Architecture
Connectivity may include serial, USB, Ethernet, BLE, Wi-Fi, or vendor-specific device interfaces for data acquisition, and Ethernet or Wi-Fi for enterprise communication. The system shall distinguish local monitoring essential functions from optional network-dependent functions such that loss of enterprise connectivity does not interrupt primary local monitoring.

### 5.7 Data Acquisition Flow
1. Detect connected sensor or data source.
2. Validate source identity and compatibility.
3. Start data stream handshake.
4. Timestamp all incoming samples using synchronized system time.
5. Perform packet integrity and sequence validation.
6. Run quality and plausibility filters.
7. Publish data to parameter and waveform processing pipelines.
8. Evaluate alarms and UI updates.
9. Persist numerical values, states, and events.
10. Export or synchronize non-blocking downstream copies where configured.

### 5.8 Processing Pipeline
Raw samples shall be normalized to canonical units, associated with device and channel identifiers, filtered for validity, and then forwarded to display and analytics modules. Derived parameters shall only be marked valid when all prerequisite source parameters meet signal-quality and timing requirements.

### 5.9 Security Architecture
The security architecture shall include user authentication, RBAC, encrypted data at rest and in transit, signed updates, secure credential storage, audit trails, session controls, and trusted device communication. Security functions shall operate without impeding timely alarm annunciation or core bedside visualization.

## 6. Functional Requirements

### 6.1 Requirement Block Format
Each requirement in this SRS includes: Requirement ID, Title, Requirement Statement, Priority, Rationale, Source, Dependencies, Verification Method, and Acceptance Criteria. Standard SRS references emphasize uniquely identified, testable requirements and clear separation of functional and non-functional content, which this format follows.[cite:5][cite:6]

### 6.2 Patient Monitoring Core

**FR-MON-001**  
**Title:** Patient Session Initialization  
**Requirement:** The system shall require creation or selection of an active patient monitoring session before associating acquired physiological data with a patient record.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Prevent incorrect patient-to-data association.  
**Source:** Inferred clinical workflow requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Session manager, user authentication, patient context module.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** No monitored patient data shall be stored under a null or ambiguous session identifier during normal operation.

**FR-MON-002**  
**Title:** Continuous Parameter Display  
**Requirement:** The application shall display all valid active monitored parameters continuously while the monitoring session is active.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Clinicians require uninterrupted visibility of current patient status.  
**Source:** Clinical monitoring workflow.  
**Dependencies:** Acquisition manager, UI dashboard, parameter engine.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Active valid parameters shall be refreshed on the primary dashboard at the configured update rate for each parameter class.

**FR-MON-003**  
**Title:** Multi-Parameter Synchronization  
**Requirement:** The system shall timestamp all acquired measurements and display synchronized parameter values using a common time base.  
**Priority:** High  
**Rationale:** Trending and derived analytics require temporal consistency.  
**Source:** Data integrity requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Time service, acquisition manager, persistence layer.  
**Verification Method:** Integration test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Sample timestamps from simultaneous sources shall not differ by more than 100 ms after synchronization adjustment.

**FR-MON-004**  
**Title:** Multi-Patient Context Protection  
**Requirement:** The system shall prevent concurrent display of data from more than one patient within a single bedside monitoring context unless an explicitly authorized multi-patient centralized view is active.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Reduce risk of patient misidentification.  
**Source:** Safety and privacy constraint.  
**Dependencies:** UI layer, session manager, RBAC.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Bedside view shall bind to only one patient context at a time, and context change shall require explicit confirmation.

### 6.3 Waveform Acquisition and Processing

**FR-WAV-001**  
**Title:** Live Waveform Rendering  
**Requirement:** The system shall render available physiological waveforms in near real time with a visual update latency not exceeding 250 ms from validated sample receipt.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Timely waveform interpretation is essential for clinician use.  
**Source:** Monitoring display requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Acquisition manager, waveform renderer, display subsystem.  
**Verification Method:** Performance test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Median end-to-end display latency shall be less than or equal to 250 ms across supported waveform channels under nominal load.

**FR-WAV-002**  
**Title:** Waveform Quality Annotation  
**Requirement:** The system shall indicate waveform quality degradation when signal quality falls below configured acceptance thresholds.  
**Priority:** High  
**Rationale:** Prevent overreliance on invalid waveforms.  
**Source:** Safety and usability.  
**Dependencies:** Signal quality engine, UI layer.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Waveform quality indicator shall transition within 1 second of signal quality threshold breach.

**FR-WAV-003**  
**Title:** Waveform Freeze and Review  
**Requirement:** The application shall allow authorized users to freeze a displayed waveform for review without interrupting background acquisition, alarm evaluation, or persistence.  
**Priority:** Medium  
**Rationale:** Supports clinical inspection without compromising monitoring continuity.  
**Source:** Clinical workflow inference.  
**Dependencies:** UI layer, acquisition manager, persistence layer.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Freeze mode shall affect only displayed view state and shall not halt acquisition or alarm processing.

### 6.4 Alarm Management Functional Requirements

**FR-ALM-001**  
**Title:** High Priority Alarm Generation  
**Requirement:** The system shall generate a high-priority audible and visual alarm when monitored physiological parameters exceed configured critical safety thresholds.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Timely clinician notification of potentially dangerous patient conditions.  
**Source:** Alarm safety requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Alarm manager, parameter engine, UI layer, audio subsystem.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Alarm annunciation shall begin within 1 second of confirmed threshold breach.

**FR-ALM-002**  
**Title:** Technical Alarm Generation  
**Requirement:** The system shall generate a technical alarm when sensor disconnect, unsupported sensor state, invalid calibration, communication timeout, or signal corruption prevents reliable monitoring.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Technical faults can invalidate patient information.  
**Source:** Sensor safety requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Sensor manager, signal quality engine, alarm manager.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Technical alarms shall be raised within 2 seconds of fault detection.

**FR-ALM-003**  
**Title:** Alarm Persistence  
**Requirement:** The system shall maintain active visual indication of an unacknowledged alarm until the alarm condition clears and alarm workflow rules permit reset.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Prevent loss of alarm awareness.  
**Source:** Alarm workflow requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Alarm manager, UI layer.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Visual alarm indication shall persist throughout active or latched alarm state.

**FR-ALM-004**  
**Title:** Alarm History Capture  
**Requirement:** The platform shall record alarm onset time, priority, source parameter, acknowledgement time, clear time, associated user action, and related technical context for each alarm event.  
**Priority:** High  
**Rationale:** Supports review, traceability, and quality investigations.  
**Source:** Audit and clinical review requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Alarm manager, audit service, database.  
**Verification Method:** Integration test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** All alarm lifecycle events shall be persisted with immutable event records.

### 6.5 Predictive Analytics and Decision Support

**FR-ANA-001**  
**Title:** Derived Analytics Availability Gating  
**Requirement:** The system shall display predictive or derived analytics only when all required source signals are present, current, and validated.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Prevent display of misleading analytics from incomplete inputs.  
**Source:** Safety and analytics integrity requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Analytics engine, signal quality engine, parameter dependency rules.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Derived analytics shall enter an unavailable state within 1 second of prerequisite loss.

**FR-ANA-002**  
**Title:** Analytics Context Display  
**Requirement:** The application shall display the validity status, update timestamp, and dependency status for each predictive analytics metric.  
**Priority:** High  
**Rationale:** Improve interpretability and reduce use error.  
**Source:** Clinical decision support usability requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Analytics engine, UI layer.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Each analytics tile shall show current status as valid, degraded, unavailable, or stale.

**FR-ANA-003**  
**Title:** Decision Support Separation  
**Requirement:** The system shall visually distinguish primary measured parameters from predictive analytics and advisory content.  
**Priority:** High  
**Rationale:** Prevent confusion between measured values and supportive interpretations.  
**Source:** Human factors and SaMD boundary requirement.  
**Dependencies:** UI design system, analytics module.  
**Verification Method:** Usability test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Measured parameters and advisory analytics shall use distinct labels and presentation regions across all supported views.

### 6.6 User Authentication and User Management

**FR-AUTH-001**  
**Title:** Named User Authentication  
**Requirement:** The application shall require named user authentication before permitting access to patient-identifiable data, configuration workflows, alarm review functions, or data export functions.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Supports security, accountability, and privacy.  
**Source:** Cybersecurity and privacy requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Identity service, RBAC, audit log service.  
**Verification Method:** Security test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Access to protected functions shall be denied until authentication is successful.

**FR-AUTH-002**  
**Title:** Role-Based Access Control  
**Requirement:** The platform shall restrict access to configuration, calibration, user management, integration settings, and export functions based on assigned user role.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Limit unsafe or unauthorized actions.  
**Source:** Security and safety requirement.  
**Dependencies:** RBAC engine, identity service, UI permissions layer.  
**Verification Method:** Security and system test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Unauthorized roles shall not be able to invoke restricted functions through the UI or API.

**FR-AUTH-003**  
**Title:** Session Reauthentication for Critical Actions

## 8. Alarm Management Requirements

### 8.1 Alarm Philosophy
The alarm subsystem shall distinguish physiological alarms, technical alarms, advisory notices, and informational notifications. Alarm behavior shall prioritize patient safety, preserve clinician awareness, and avoid masking unresolved technical conditions.

### 8.2 Alarm Priorities
- Priority 1 - Critical: Immediate patient safety risk or loss of essential monitoring integrity.
- Priority 2 - High: Significant clinical concern requiring prompt attention.
- Priority 3 - Medium: Condition requiring review but not immediate response.
- Priority 4 - Low/Advisory: Non-urgent condition or predictive advisory.

### 8.3 Alarm Requirements

**ALM-001**  
**Title:** Alarm Priority Resolution  
**Requirement:** The system shall assign each alarm to a configured priority class based on parameter, threshold, technical cause, and monitoring mode.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Required for consistent notification behavior.  
**Source:** Alarm management policy.  
**Dependencies:** Alarm rules engine, parameter engine, monitoring mode manager.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** For each configured alarm condition, the assigned priority shall match the approved alarm rules configuration.

**ALM-002**  
**Title:** Alarm Escalation  
**Requirement:** The system shall escalate an unacknowledged active high or critical alarm according to configured escalation timers and notification rules.  
**Priority:** High  
**Rationale:** Reduce risk of ignored alarm conditions.  
**Source:** Safety and workflow requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Alarm manager, notification service, timing service.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Escalation shall occur within configured timer tolerance of plus or minus 1 second.

**ALM-003**  
**Title:** Audible Silence Behavior  
**Requirement:** The system shall permit authorized users to silence audible alarm output for a configured interval while maintaining persistent visual alarm indication and alarm state logging.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Support procedure workflow without losing alarm awareness.  
**Source:** Clinical alarm workflow.  
**Dependencies:** Alarm manager, UI layer, audit service, audio subsystem.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Visual alarm and countdown to silence expiration shall remain visible throughout the silence interval.

**ALM-004**  
**Title:** Alarm Pause Restrictions  
**Requirement:** The system shall restrict alarm pause functionality to authorized roles, defined monitoring modes, and configured maximum pause durations.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Uncontrolled alarm pause can create hazardous silent states.  
**Source:** Safety requirement.  
**Dependencies:** RBAC, alarm policy engine, monitoring mode manager.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Alarm pause shall terminate automatically at timeout or when disallowed state transitions occur.

**ALM-005**  
**Title:** Re-trigger Conditions  
**Requirement:** The system shall re-trigger audible alarm annunciation when an alarmed condition worsens, recurs after clear, or remains active beyond the configured silence interval.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Prevent prolonged unattended abnormal states.  
**Source:** Alarm safety logic.  
**Dependencies:** Alarm manager, timing service, parameter engine.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Re-trigger behavior shall follow configured severity and timer rules for each alarm class.

**ALM-006**  
**Title:** Alarm Synchronization  
**Requirement:** The platform shall synchronize active alarm state, acknowledgement state, and clear state across all local modules and authorized connected viewers within 2 seconds of state change.  
**Priority:** High  
**Rationale:** Ensure consistent user awareness.  
**Source:** Distributed monitoring requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Alarm manager, messaging service, UI clients.  
**Verification Method:** Integration test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** State divergence among synchronized clients shall not exceed 2 seconds.

### 8.4 Textual Alarm State Diagram
```text
[Normal]
  -> threshold breach / technical fault
[Alarm Pending Validation]
  -> confirmed condition
[Active Unacknowledged Alarm]
  -> user acknowledge
[Active Acknowledged Alarm]
  -> condition clears
[Cleared / Logged]

From Active Unacknowledged or Active Acknowledged:
  -> audible silence requested -> [Visual Active + Audible Silenced]
  -> pause allowed and initiated -> [Paused with Countdown]
  -> condition worsens -> [Escalated Alarm]
  -> technical invalidation of source -> [Technical Alarm Override]
```

### 8.5 Safety Implications
- Technical alarm states shall override parameter validity states where measurement integrity is compromised.
- Derived analytics alarms shall not suppress primary physiological threshold alarms.
- Alarm silence shall never erase visual evidence of active alarm conditions.
- When audio hardware fails self-test, the system shall generate a technical fault and require alternative visual emphasis.

### 9.3 Parameter-Specific Requirement Pattern

**MON-HR-001**  
**Title:** Heart Rate Display and Validation  
**Requirement:** The system shall display heart rate only when a valid source signal designated by the source-priority configuration is present and passes signal-quality validation.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Prevent display of unreliable pulse information.  
**Source:** Core monitoring requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Signal quality engine, parameter engine, source manager.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** HR shall transition to unavailable state within 2 seconds of loss of valid source.

**MON-BP-001**  
**Title:** Blood Pressure Availability  
**Requirement:** The platform shall display invasive blood pressure continuously when an invasive pressure source is active and validated, and shall display non-invasive blood pressure results upon completion of a valid cuff cycle when NIBP mode is active.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** BP is a primary hemodynamic variable.  
**Source:** Clinical monitoring workflow.  
**Dependencies:** Pressure sensor interface, NIBP interface, parameter engine.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Source type and timestamp shall be indicated for displayed BP values.

**MON-MAP-001**  
**Title:** MAP Calculation and Display  
**Requirement:** The system shall calculate or receive MAP and display it as a primary parameter whenever blood pressure data are valid and sufficient for MAP determination.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** MAP is required for advanced hemodynamic and safety logic.  
**Source:** Hemodynamic requirement.  
**Dependencies:** BP module, parameter engine.  
**Verification Method:** Unit and system test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** MAP shall update whenever the upstream BP source updates.

**MON-CO-001**  
**Title:** Cardiac Output Gating  
**Requirement:** The application shall display cardiac output only when the designated CO measurement or calculation module reports valid state and all prerequisites are satisfied.  
**Priority:** High  
**Rationale:** Advanced metrics must not be displayed without validity assurance.  
**Source:** Advanced hemodynamic requirement.  
**Dependencies:** CO module, signal quality engine, analytics dependencies.  
**Verification Method:** Integration test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** CO shall show unavailable state with reason code when prerequisites fail.

**MON-SPO2-001**  
**Title:** SpO2 Signal Quality Handling  
**Requirement:** The system shall suppress alarm evaluation based on SpO2 values when the associated pulse oximetry signal quality indicates invalid measurement state and shall instead raise a technical or quality alarm as configured.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Avoid false physiological alarms from poor perfusion or sensor artifact.  
**Source:** Safety and signal integrity requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Pulse oximetry interface, signal quality engine, alarm manager.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Alarm source shall transition from physiological evaluation to technical quality indication within 2 seconds of invalid quality state.

**MON-ETCO2-001**  
**Title:** EtCO2 Value and Waveform Correlation  
**Requirement:** The system shall display EtCO2 numeric values with the corresponding capnography waveform source and shall invalidate the numeric value when the waveform source is lost or flagged invalid.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Numerical interpretation depends on source waveform integrity.  
**Source:** Respiratory monitoring safety requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Capnography interface, waveform engine, parameter engine.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Numeric EtCO2 shall not remain valid longer than 2 seconds after waveform invalidation.

**MON-BIS-001**  
**Title:** Sedation Metric Context Display  
**Requirement:** The system shall display processed sedation metrics, including BIS-equivalent indices where supported, together with signal quality, update timestamp, and artifact status.  
**Priority:** High  
**Rationale:** Interpretation requires context regarding artifact and source validity.  
**Source:** Neuro-monitoring requirement.  
**Dependencies:** EEG/EMG module, analytics engine, UI layer.  
**Verification Method:** Integration and usability test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Artifact or poor-quality status shall be visible whenever the sedation metric is degraded.

Additional parameter-specific requirements shall be instantiated for SV, SVV, PPV, StO2, DO2, HPI, GHI, AFM, CAI, respiratory rate, temperature, and EEG/EMG channels following the same requirement pattern during detailed design baselining.

## 14. Safety Requirements

**Requirement:** The system shall transition to a defined safe state when a critical internal fault prevents reliable acquisition, alarm processing, or display of essential monitoring information.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Prevent misleading clinical operation under unsafe fault conditions.  
**Source:** System safety requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Fault manager, watchdog, UI layer, alarm manager.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Safe state shall visibly announce the fault, invalidate affected data, and prevent continued presentation of compromised values as current.

**SAF-002**

**Requirement:** The platform shall implement watchdog supervision for critical processes including acquisition, alarm evaluation, and UI heartbeat monitoring.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Detect process hang conditions.  
**Source:** Embedded safety requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Watchdog framework, process manager.  
**Verification Method:** Integration test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Failure of supervised processes shall be detected and logged within the configured watchdog timeout.

**SAF-003**  
**Title:** Power Loss Handling  
**Requirement:** The system shall preserve persisted records, close open transactions safely, and restore to controlled startup behavior after recoverable power interruption.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Prevent data corruption and ambiguous restart state.  
**Source:** Safety and reliability requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Persistence layer, startup manager, power monitor.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Simulated abrupt power loss shall not result in undetected database corruption in validated recovery tests.

**SAF-004**  
**Title:** Sensor Failure Safety Handling  
**Requirement:** The system shall invalidate any parameter dependent on a failed or disconnected sensor and shall initiate the corresponding technical alarm workflow.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Prevent stale or fabricated measurements.  
**Source:** Sensor safety requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Sensor manager, parameter engine, alarm manager.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** All dependent parameters shall be invalidated within 2 seconds of confirmed sensor failure.

## 15. Data Management Requirements

**DAT-001**  
**Title:** Trend Retention  
**Requirement:** The system shall retain numeric trend data for the active and completed monitoring sessions for a minimum configurable duration of 30 days on validated local storage profiles.  
**Priority:** High  
**Rationale:** Support review and analysis.  
**Source:** Data management requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Database, retention manager.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Requested trend review data within retention window shall be retrievable without loss of timestamps or units.

**DAT-002**  
**Title:** Patient Session Closure  
**Requirement:** The application shall require explicit session closure or transfer workflow before permitting a patient context to be removed from active monitoring.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Prevent accidental orphaning of active patient data.  
**Source:** Clinical workflow requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Session manager, UI layer, audit service.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Session closure shall create an auditable event and finalize session records.

**DAT-003**  
**Title:** HL7/FHIR Observation Export  
**Requirement:** The system shall support export of configured observations and device-generated records using site-enabled HL7 and/or FHIR interface profiles where integration is enabled.  
**Priority:** Medium  
**Rationale:** Enable enterprise interoperability.  
**Source:** Interoperability requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Integration service, mapping engine, RBAC.  
**Verification Method:** Integration test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Exported messages shall conform to configured profile mappings and include unit and timestamp fields.

**DAT-004**  
**Title:** Backup and Restore  
**Requirement:** The platform shall support controlled backup and validated restore of configuration, audit logs, and retained session records without altering source timestamps or event order.  
**Priority:** Medium  
**Rationale:** Support resilience and investigations.  
**Source:** Operational support requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Backup service, storage manager, security service.  
**Verification Method:** Recovery test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Restored datasets shall pass integrity verification and remain queryable.

## 16. Validation and Verification Strategy

### 16.1 V&V Principles
Verification planning shall cover unit, integration, system, usability, performance, cybersecurity, and traceability validation activities. Standard SRS practice emphasizes testable requirements; accordingly, each requirement category in this document includes an intended verification method to support downstream V&V planning.[cite:5][cite:6]

### 16.2 Verification Requirements

**VAL-001**  
**Title:** Unit Test Coverage for Safety-Critical Logic  
**Requirement:** The development process shall define and execute unit tests for safety-critical alarm logic, parameter validation logic, and session-state transition logic with documented expected results.  
**Priority:** High  
**Rationale:** Early defect detection.  
**Source:** Verification strategy requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Development framework, CI pipeline.  
**Verification Method:** Process audit and review.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Safety-critical units shall have documented unit tests covering nominal, boundary, and error conditions.

**VAL-002**  
**Title:** Integration Testing of Sensor and Analytics Interfaces  
**Requirement:** Integration tests shall verify correct data flow, timing, and fault handling among sensor adapters, acquisition services, calculation engines, and alarm services.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Inter-module failures can compromise monitoring integrity.  
**Source:** Integration requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Integration environment, simulators, test harness.  
**Verification Method:** Integration test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** All supported interface combinations in the approved test matrix shall pass defined data and fault scenarios.

**VAL-003**  
**Title:** Alarm Verification  
**Requirement:** System testing shall verify alarm thresholds, delay logic, silence behavior, pause behavior, escalation, latching, and history recording for each configured alarm class.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Alarm correctness is essential performance.  
**Source:** Alarm verification requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Alarm test matrix, simulator inputs, system test environment.  
**Verification Method:** System test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** All alarm scenarios shall demonstrate behavior matching approved alarm rules within specified timing tolerances.

**VAL-004**  
**Title:** Clinical Simulation Testing  
**Requirement:** The verification strategy shall include representative clinical simulation scenarios covering OR, ICU, sedation, and technical fault workflows.  
**Priority:** High  
**Rationale:** Validate end-to-end workflow realism.  
**Source:** Clinical workflow and usability requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Simulation environment, clinical review, test protocols.  
**Verification Method:** Clinical simulation test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Simulated workflow scenarios shall execute without unresolved safety-critical deviations.

### 16.3 Test Environment Requirements
- Hardware-in-the-loop environment for sensor adapters.
- Data replay environment for waveform and numerical scenarios.
- Isolated cybersecurity test environment.
- UI usability evaluation environment with representative lighting and noise.
- Traceability-controlled verification repository.

### 16.4 Entry and Exit Criteria
- Entry: approved baselined requirements, version-controlled test objects, calibrated simulators, and approved test protocols.
- Exit: all critical and high-priority defects dispositioned, traceability complete, deviations approved, and test evidence archived.

## 19. Software Architecture Requirements

### 19.1 Layered Architecture
The software shall be partitioned into at least the following layers:
- Hardware Abstraction Layer.
- Acquisition and Device Communication Layer.
- Signal Quality and Processing Layer.
- Parameter and Analytics Computation Layer.
- Alarm and Rules Layer.
- Persistence and Audit Layer.
- Integration and Export Layer.
- UI Presentation Layer.
- Security and Identity Layer.

### 19.2 Architecture Requirements

**ARC-001**  
**Title:** Module Interface Definition  
**Requirement:** Each software module shall expose documented interfaces defining inputs, outputs, error states, timing expectations, and ownership boundaries.  
**Priority:** High  
**Rationale:** Enables controlled integration and verification.  
**Source:** Software architecture requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Architecture description, interface control documents.  
**Verification Method:** Design review.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Each baselined module shall have an approved interface definition record.

**ARC-002**  
**Title:** Fault Containment  
**Requirement:** The architecture shall isolate non-essential failures such that failure of optional analytics, export, or synchronization modules does not block essential local monitoring and alarm processing.  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Rationale:** Preserve essential monitoring functions.  
**Source:** Safety and availability requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Process architecture, watchdog, fault manager.  
**Verification Method:** Integration and fault-injection test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Injected failure of non-essential modules shall not interrupt core acquisition and alarm functionality.

**ARC-003**  
**Title:** Canonical Data Model  
**Requirement:** The platform shall normalize incoming parameter data into a canonical internal model including source identifier, units, timestamp, validity state, quality state, and patient session identifier.  
**Priority:** High  
**Rationale:** Prevent inconsistencies across modules.  
**Source:** Data architecture requirement.  
**Dependencies:** Acquisition manager, parameter engine, persistence layer.  
**Verification Method:** Integration test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Internal messages shall include the mandatory canonical fields for all monitored data classes.

**ARC-004**  
**Title:** API Version Control  
**Requirement:** Internal and external service interfaces shall support version identification and backward compatibility strategy documentation.  
**Priority:** Medium  
**Rationale:** Reduce integration breakage during iteration.  
**Source:** Software lifecycle best practice.  
**Dependencies:** Integration services, architecture governance.  
**Verification Method:** Design review and interface test.  
**Acceptance Criteria:** Every released API endpoint or message schema shall include version metadata.

## 20. Appendix

### 20.1 Assumptions
- Advanced analytics names such as HPI, GHI, AFM, and CAI are treated as configurable algorithmic modules whose exact mathematical definitions are product-package dependent.
- The proof-of-concept may use simulated or bridged sensors for selected parameters.
- Final marketed claims and accuracy statements are outside this document unless supported by separate validation evidence.
- Hospital integration is optional for the initial prototype and shall not be prerequisite to local monitoring.

### 20.2 Open Issues
- Final supported sensor vendor matrix to be baselined.
- Final hardware platform selection and screen size constraints to be baselined.
- Final alarm tones and alarm philosophy approval pending clinical review.
- Final privacy deployment profile for HIPAA-only, GDPR-only, or dual-jurisdiction environments pending deployment strategy.
- Final algorithm availability and indication boundaries for predictive metrics pending analytical validation.

### 20.3 Future Enhancements
- Multi-bed central station support.
- Remote clinician notification applications.
- Enhanced clinical pathway guidance.
- AI-assisted waveform interpretation with explainability controls.
- Integrated report generation and longitudinal analytics.
- Advanced fleet management and remote update orchestration.

### 20.4 Example Alarm Sequence
1. Invasive MAP falls below configured critical threshold.
2. Signal quality remains valid for confirmation delay.
3. Alarm manager transitions to active critical physiological alarm.
4. Audible tone and red visual banner are activated.
5. Event is logged with timestamp and source values.
6. User acknowledges alarm; visual state persists while condition remains active.
7. MAP recovers within normal range for configured clear interval.
8. Alarm clears and history record is finalized.

### 20.5 Example UI Workflow
1. User authenticates.
2. User selects patient and confirms session start.
3. User connects invasive pressure source and pulse oximetry sensor.
4. System validates sensors and updates dashboard status.
5. User enters advanced hemodynamic mode.
6. Dashboard displays MAP, CO, CI, SVV, PPV, and HPI tiles with dependency states.
7. Alarm occurs and overlay takes focus.
8. User reviews trend screen and acknowledges alarm.
9. Session data are exported after session closure by authorized role.

### 20.6 Example Sensor Lifecycle
1. Sensor detected.
2. Sensor type validated.
3. Channel mapped.
4. Calibration required and completed.
5. Signal quality validated.
6. Parameter enabled.
7. Fault or disconnect occurs.
8. Parameter invalidated and technical alarm generated.
9. Sensor replaced.
10. Revalidation completed.
11. Parameter restored.

### 20.7 Writing and Review Checklist
- All requirements shall remain atomic, measurable, and testable.
- Critical tasks shall be linked to usability evaluation.
- Risk controls shall be traceable to hazards and verification evidence.
- Parameter validity dependencies shall be explicitly documented during detailed design.
- All interface schemas shall be baselined before integration verification begins.
- All assumptions converted to design decisions shall be version controlled and approved.
